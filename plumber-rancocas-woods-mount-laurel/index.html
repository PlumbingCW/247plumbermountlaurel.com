A community that predates most of the township's suburbs

Rancocas Woods sits on Rancocas Creek and is reached from I-295 Exit 43. Its first houses went up in the 1930s on the site of a former amusement park, while most of Mount Laurel grew after the late 1960s. The Creek Road shopping strip is its local center.

What that means for you is simple. The age of the neighborhood does not tell a plumber what is inside your walls. Your own house does, so the visit starts with looking at the pipe where it shows in a basement, crawl space or utility closet. Plumbers work across Mount Laurel, and The Woods is one of the stops.

Three kinds of house, three places to start

Listing sites describe log cabins, ranch homes with carports and larger colonial or contemporary houses, often on lots near half an acre.

Larger homes

Storm outages and the pumps that depend on power

Documented in August 2011

Irene's late August 2011 storm left power out for a long stretch in Rancocas Woods, and also in Ramblewood, Masonville, Canterbury and Fellowship. The township utilities authority kept its sewage pumps running on generators. Damage township-wide was minimal.

If you have a basement sump pump, a long outage is its real test. Lift the float now and again, and ask a plumber about a battery backup if the pump matters to you. A pump that fails is a sump pump repair call, ideally made before the storm.

Sewer or septic: confirm it, do not assume it

Early residents here had no sewers, according to the community's history, and flooding in heavy rain was part of that story. Whether your street is on public sewer today is a fact to confirm with the Mount Laurel utilities authority, not one to guess from the age of the area.

It matters because the fix differs. A public-sewer blockage in your lateral responds to drain cleaning or a cable, while a septic tank problem does not. Tell the plumber which one you have, or say you are not sure and they will look for the cleanout.

What a call from The Woods looks like

A clear call gets the right plumber to the right house the first time.

  1. Give the address. Name your street and the nearest main road, such as Creek Road, since some addresses are hard to find.
  2. Say what you see. Describe the water, where it is, and whether it is clear, brown or smells like sewage.
  3. Shut off what you can. Close the fixture valve or the main if water is spreading, and keep power away from wet areas.
  4. Ask about the plan. Get the likely cause and the next step explained before any repair starts.

Water heaters on the creek side of town

A water heater that leaks at the base or trips constantly is a common reason to call. A water heater replacement makes sense when the tank itself is leaking, and a repair makes sense when it is only an element or thermostat.

Ask the plumber to show you the data plate on the tank for its age, and decide from there instead of from a guess.
