Laurel Creek, off Union Mill Road

Laurel Creek is a residential subdivision in Mount Laurel Township, reached from Union Mill Road. Listing sites say Orleans built it around 1991, with condos and townhomes. This page is about the Laurel Creek in Mount Laurel, not other communities with a similar name in nearby towns.

Mount Laurel is the place to start on the township's plumbing coverage, and Laurel Creek homes share one trait: most are attached, so a problem next door can become your problem.

Condos and townhomes, and where trouble tends to show

Listing sites describe the condos as about 900 to 1,300 square feet with no garage or basement, and the townhomes as about 1,000 to over 2,300 square feet, some with basements and attached garages.

Condos with no basement

A leak shows up inside the unit or in the one below. Check the water heater closet and the ceiling under any upstairs unit first.

Townhomes with a basement

Look at the floor near the heater and any floor drain. A basement tells you sooner than a slab does.

Hard water and your faucets and heater

Public water in Mount Laurel runs from moderately hard to hard at the high end, and the supply can be a mix of sources. Both the township utilities authority and New Jersey American Water serve parts of town, so ask which one bills your unit.

Hardness leaves white scale on aerators and shower heads and builds sediment in a tank. If a faucet sputters, soaking the aerator in vinegar often fixes it. A noisy water heater points to sediment, and a water heater repair visit can flush it or test the elements.

Water near electrical: what to do first

If water is dripping near a light fixture, outlet or the electrical panel, stay clear and switch off the affected circuit only if you can reach the breaker safely and dry. Close the water supply, then call a plumber, and an electrician if the wiring got wet.

Do not touch a wet outlet or stand in standing water to reach the panel. The plumber can stop the water, but the wiring is a separate trade.

Basements, floor drains and the lowest point in a townhome

Townhomes with a basement have a floor drain at the lowest point of the house. If that drain gurgles or backs up, the blockage is probably in the line leaving your home or in the municipal main. Stop running water until a plumber has looked, because every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

A camera look at the line shows whether roots, grease or a collapsed section is behind it. That is a sewer inspection, and it gives you something concrete to show management or the township if the problem is outside your property.

Toilets, drains and installs

A toilet that clogs more than once a month points to a drain issue, not bad luck. A toilet repair checks the flapper, the fill valve and the seal under the bowl. A slow drain in several fixtures calls for drain cleaning or a camera look at the line.

When you replace a fixture or add a laundry hookup, a plumbing installation visit begins with how the existing lines run. In a condo, that also means checking whether the work needs association approval.
