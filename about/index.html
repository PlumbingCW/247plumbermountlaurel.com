About Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing in Mount Laurel, NJ

We are a 24/7 dispatch line that connects Mount Laurel homes and businesses with licensed plumbers. This page explains how that works and where it stops.

What this service is, in plain terms

Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing is a dispatch service. A person answers the phone day and night, listens to the problem and sends a licensed plumber to your address in Mount Laurel or a nearby town. Our address is 305 Fellowship Rd, Mount Laurel, NJ 08054, and the phone number is (609) 547-1417.

We run on phone calls only. There is no form to fill in and no app to install, because a burst pipe or a sewage backup is not the moment to type into a screen. Pick up the phone and talk to a dispatcher.

What we cover

The work falls into three groups, and the dispatcher will tell you which one your call belongs to.

Urgent repairs

Burst pipes, leaks, sewage backups, failed water heaters, toilets and faucets that will not stop running. See our full service list for the details.

Planned work

Water heater installation, repiping, sewer camera inspections and new fixtures, booked for a time that suits you.

Commercial calls

Offices, shops and light industrial space, including the business parks along Fellowship Road near I-295 and the Turnpike.

Nearby towns

Moorestown, Cherry Hill, Evesham, Medford and Mount Holly all have their own pages. Look at the service area page to see how far the line reaches.

How a call works from the first ring

Here is the order of events so there are no surprises.

  1. You call and a dispatcher answers. Give your address, the town and a short description of what is wrong. If you are in the middle of a leak, say so first.
  2. The dispatcher asks a few questions. Where is the water, which fixtures are affected, and can you reach the shutoff valve. These answers help the plumber bring the right parts.
  3. A licensed plumber is sent. The plumber who responds is the one who does the work. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic and earlier calls change the schedule, but the dispatcher will tell you what to expect.
  4. The price comes before the work. The plumber looks at the problem, explains the options in ordinary words and gives a clear price. If you say no, you say no.
  5. The work is done and explained. You see what was found and what was changed, and you can ask for old parts back.

Not sure whether your problem is urgent? Call the 24/7 line and ask the dispatcher.

How to check that any New Jersey plumber is licensed

New Jersey licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the Division of Consumer Affairs. The board offers an online license lookup. You can use it to check any plumber you plan to hire, including the one we send.

Ask the plumber for the license details when they arrive and look them up yourself. A real professional will not mind. We do not print license details on this site, because the official lookup is the source you should trust, not a web page.

The standards the trade follows in this state

New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code plumbing subcode adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. In practice that governs things like pipe sizing, venting, backflow protection and how a water heater relief valve is piped. The plumbing subcode official in your town is the authority that enforces it.

Before a bigger job, ask the Mount Laurel construction office what applies such as a repipe or a water heater swap. A plumber can tell you what the work involves, but the town decides what needs a permit.

What we do not do

A plumber is the right call for water, drains and fixtures. Some problems belong to someone else, and we will say so.

  • Gas utility repairs. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and the gas utility from outside.
  • Electrical work. If water is near a panel or outlets, a licensed electrician is the right person.
  • Structural repair. We can stop the water, but fixing a rotted joist or a sagging ceiling is a carpenter's job.
  • Insurance decisions. We can describe what we found, but your policy and your agent decide what is covered.
  • Work without a clear price. If the plumber cannot explain the job in plain words, ask again before saying yes.

Admitting these limits is part of working honestly. It is better to refer you than to start a job outside the trade.
