Birchfield, the lakes and what is nearby

Birchfield is a named place inside Mount Laurel Township, with lakes at its center and walkways around them. Listing sites place Mount Laurel Memorial Park to the north, Laurel Acres Park to the south, and the Town Square shopping center within a block.

Most homes here are condos, with townhouses and patio homes beside them and a handful of single-family houses. Shared walls and shared stacks shape almost every plumbing call, so the first question is always whose pipe it is. Plumbers work across Mount Laurel, and Birchfield is within easy reach.

Condo leak? Five things that save time and money

  • Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the unit's main as soon as water appears.
  • Tell the neighbor above or below, since their unit may be the source or the victim.
  • Photograph the damage with a date before cleanup starts.
  • Call management early, because shared lines are theirs to approve.
  • Ask the plumber for a written note of the cause and the fix for your insurance file.

From the first call to a finished condo repair

A clear sequence keeps a small problem from turning into a dispute with a neighbor.

  1. Stop the water. Close the fixture valve or the unit's main, and move valuables away from the wet area.
  2. Describe the location. Say which room, which floor, and whether a neighbor's unit is involved.
  3. Let the plumber find the source. A meter test or a look behind the access panel tells you whether it is a supply line, a drain or a fixture.
  4. Decide who pays. With the cause documented, you and management can sort out who owns the repair.

Faucets, toilets and small repairs that add up

Many Birchfield calls are small: a faucet that drips, a toilet that keeps running, or a fixture that needs replacing. A dripping tap wastes water all day, and faucet repair is usually a cartridge or washer. A running toilet is often a worn flapper, and toilet repair fixes it in one visit.

In a condo, a running toilet that overflows can reach the unit below, so do not let it ride.

Water heaters in tight closets

Many condos tuck the water heater into a utility closet. A tank that leaks there can soak a carpet or damage a neighboring wall before you notice, so check the base now and then. A water heater replacement in a closet starts with measuring the opening, the venting and the shutoff access.

Ask the plumber about a drain pan and a leak alarm. They cost little compared with a flooded closet.

A toilet that clogs once is a plunger problem, and one that clogs weekly is a line problem. Do not keep forcing it, because a repeated overflow can reach the ceiling of the unit below.
