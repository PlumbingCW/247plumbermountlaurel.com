Plumbing problems fall into two piles. One pile is damaging your home right now, like a burst pipe, a sewage backup or a tank that has split. The other pile is work you can schedule, like a new water heater or a camera look at the sewer line before you buy a house.

Urgent or can it wait, a quick way to decide

Treat it as urgent if water is running and you cannot stop it, if sewage is coming up through a drain, or if you have no water at all. A leaking tank is urgent too, since a small drip at the base can turn into a flood. See burst pipe repair and sewer line cleaning for what a plumber does first.

A slow drain in one sink, a faucet drip or a toilet that runs after flushing can usually wait for a daytime visit. Even then, a repair that waits a month can cost more in water, so the sooner the better. For hot water that has gone cold, check the breaker or pilot, then read about water heater repair.

Planned work works the other way around. You choose the date. If you are weighing a tank against a tankless unit, our tankless water heater page lays out the trade-offs, and repiping explains when a whole-house job beats a patch. If you are unsure, call and describe it, and the dispatcher will say which pile it belongs in.
