What Larchmont means locally

Larchmont is a name Mount Laurel residents use for an area that includes Larchmont Estates, Larchmont Elementary School and the Larchmont shopping plazas. Listing sites place the plazas about a mile south of Rancocas Woods.

We found no official boundary, so give your street name when you call. Plumbers work across Mount Laurel, and a street name is enough to send them to the right place.

Mid-size family homes and what they ask of a water heater

Listing sites describe Larchmont Estates as mid-size homes built in the mid 1980s, many with four bedrooms and three or more bathrooms, alongside some attached homes. A house like that puts a lot of demand on one tank at the same time.

If two showers and the dishwasher run at once and the water turns cold, the heater may be undersized or failing. Ask a plumber about capacity before you buy. A water heater replacement is a good time to look at a tankless water heater too.

Upstairs bathrooms and slow leaks

A leak under an upstairs bathroom often shows first as a stain on the ceiling below. More bathrooms mean more supply lines, drains and wax seals that can fail. A toilet repair often solves a bowl that rocks or weeps at the base.

If the stain keeps growing and no fixture is dripping, a leak detection survey finds the source before a wall or ceiling is opened. Shut off the main if the water is spreading.

While you wait for the plumber to arrive

  1. Find the shutoff. Know where the main valve is and turn it a quarter turn each year so it does not seize.
  2. Clear the area. Move rugs, boxes and stored items away from the wet spot so the plumber can reach it.
  3. Note the symptoms. Write down when it started, which fixtures are affected and whether the water is clear.
  4. Keep kids and pets away. Wet floors and standing water are slip hazards, and sewage is a health hazard.

Hard water and Larchmont's fixtures

What the township reports

The township reports hardness ranging from 63 to 138 parts per million, about 3.7 to 8.1 grains per gallon. The water comes from the Mount Laurel utilities authority or, in part of town, New Jersey American Water.

That is moderately hard to hard at the high end, so expect scale on shower heads and sediment in a tank. Ask which utility serves your street, and flush the heater once a year.

Drains in a busy household

Family homes put hair, grease and wipes into drains every day. A single slow sink responds to drain cleaning. If several fixtures slow together, the clog sits in the main line and needs a cable or a camera.

A garbage disposal that jams or a washer that drains slowly can also point to the same main line, so tell the plumber everything you have noticed.
