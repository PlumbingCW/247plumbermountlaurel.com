When the tank gives out and the floor is wet

Water heater replacement means taking out a failed tank or tankless unit and setting a new one with the water, fuel, venting and safety connections done to current practice. If yours is leaking, cold, or rumbling like a kettle, call the 24/7 line. The 24/7 line is staffed nights, weekends and holidays, and same-day appointments are available when units are on hand.

While you wait, close the cold water valve at the top of the tank and shut off its fuel. On a gas unit that is the gas control knob, and on an electric unit it is the breaker. Leave the drain valve closed and keep people and pets out of any pooled water near a plugged-in appliance.

A leaking tank is a replacement, not a repair

Water that seeps from the bottom or the seam of the steel tank means the inner lining has rusted through. No patch, sealant or tightened bolt fixes that, and it will only get worse. If the water is coming from a fitting, a valve or the relief pipe instead, the tank may still be sound, and the plumber will check before recommending a new one.

Gas, electric or heat pump for the swap

Most homeowners keep the fuel they have, because switching means new gas line, venting or a new circuit. The table shows what each choice asks of the house.

Going from gas to a heat pump is a bigger project than a like for like swap, so ask for it as a planned job if the old tank is still holding water.

What the plumber does on swap day

A standard tank swap follows the same order whether it is a Saturday night or a weekday morning.

  1. Confirm the diagnosis. The plumber checks that the leak really is the tank and not a fitting or the relief valve line.
  2. Shut off and drain. Water, gas or power are isolated, and the old tank is drained to a floor drain or outside with a hose.
  3. Disconnect and haul out. Supply lines, flue and gas connector come off, and the old unit is carried out so the pan area can be dried and checked.
  4. Set and connect the new unit. New flexible connectors, a dielectric fitting where needed, a fresh relief valve and a drain pan go in, and the tank is leveled.
  5. Vent and test. A gas unit gets its flue checked for draft and a combustion test. An electric unit is tested for voltage at the thermostat.
  6. Fill, purge and set the temperature. The tank is filled with a hot tap open to purge air, then set to a safe temperature and left running while the plumber confirms no drips.

Sizing the new tank to the people in the house

Tank size is only half the story. The number that matters is the first hour rating on the yellow label, which tells you how much hot water the unit can deliver in a busy hour. A household of one or two often does fine on a 40 gallon tank, while a family of four with teenagers usually wants 50 gallons or more.

Tell the dispatcher how many people shower in the morning and whether you run a soaking tub. If your old unit always ran out, go up a size or look at a higher recovery model. If it never ran out, matching it is usually the sensible and cheaper call.

Hard water and the crust inside Mount Laurel tanks

The township's water quality summary lists 63 to 138 ppm across the supplies it names. The MUA and New Jersey American Water sit near 80 to 100 ppm. That full span is roughly 3.7 to 8.1 grains per gallon, moderately hard to hard at the high end. Over time that hardness settles as scale on electric elements and as a layer of sediment on the bottom of a gas tank. Both slow recovery and shorten the tank's life.

Most of the township's housing was built after the mid 1960s, so many heaters here are second or third replacements on the same plumbing. Ask the plumber to flush the sediment on the new tank every year or so and to check the anode rod, which sacrifices itself to protect the steel.

Relief valve, expansion tank and venting questions

Every new tank gets a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe run to a safe spot, never capped or plugged. If your house has a check valve or pressure reducing valve on the water service, heated water has nowhere to expand, and an expansion tank takes up the extra volume. Without one, the relief valve can drip every time the burner runs.

A gas unit must vent combustion gases outdoors through a flue sized for that model. Atmospheric flues rely on natural draft, while power vent and direct vent models use a fan and plastic pipe. Permit rules vary, so ask the Mount Laurel construction office whether your swap needs one, and know that New Jersey adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code.

Water heater flooding the basement tonight

Water heaters fail at 2 a.m. as often as noon. Call the 24/7 line any night, weekend or holiday, and a dispatcher takes your address and the type of unit.

  1. Close the cold supply. The valve is on the pipe entering the top of the tank. Turn the handle until it stops.
  2. Cut the fuel. Turn the gas control to off, or switch the dedicated breaker off, before touching anything wet.
  3. Smell gas? Leave. Go outside, call 911 and the gas utility from outside, then call us. Do not flip switches on the way out.
  4. Move belongings up. Lift boxes and electronics off the floor and open a drain or run a wet vacuum if it is safe.
  5. Photograph the unit. Snap the rating label, the pooled water and any damage, because your insurer may ask.

If water is spreading past the utility room and you cannot find its source, see leak detection to trace the line.

What moves the price of a new water heater

Two replacements on the same street can cost very different amounts, and the unit itself is only part of it.

  • Fuel and type. A heat pump or tankless model costs more to buy and set than a standard gas or electric tank.
  • Size and efficiency. Bigger tanks and higher efficiency ratings raise the equipment price.
  • Venting changes. A flue that no longer meets the new unit's needs, or a switch to power vent, adds parts and labor.
  • Code upgrades. An expansion tank, a drain pan or a new relief valve line adds to the job.
  • Access and haul-away. Attic or tight closet installs, long stair carries and disposal of the old tank take extra time.

Insurance may respond to a sudden flood from a burst tank and not to the tank itself, so photograph the wet area and the data plate before the old unit leaves.

What to do after the new heater is in

Write the install date and the model number on the tank with a marker, and keep the paperwork where you can find it. If you ever need warranty help, the serial number and install date will be the first things asked. Set the thermostat to a temperature that is safe for children, and check the relief pipe for drips over the first few days.

If a pipe fitting nearby looks green or weepy, mention it before the plumber leaves. Older copper in the township's 1970s and 1980s homes can pinhole at the same time as the tank, and a quick look now saves a second emergency call.
