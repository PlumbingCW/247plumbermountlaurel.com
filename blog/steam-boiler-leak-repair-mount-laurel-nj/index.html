Steam Boiler Leak Repair in Mount Laurel: A Homeowner Guide

Steam boilers are less common in newer homes, but plenty of older houses still rely on them. A leak can be a small weep or a sign the boiler has to be shut down today.

By the Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 9, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026.

Call (609) 547-1417

First, make sure you really have a steam boiler

A steam boiler heats water into steam that rises through pipes to radiators and then condenses back to water. You will usually see a glass tube on the side, called a sight glass, that shows the water level, and a low pressure gauge that stays near 1 to 2 psi. A hot water boiler has no sight glass and runs at a higher pressure with circulator pumps.

This matters because the repair path is different. If you are not sure which you have, look for the sight glass and check the rating plate for the word steam.

Shut it down if you see any of these

Turn off the boiler at its emergency switch if water is spraying, the floor around it is soaking wet, the relief valve is discharging or you smell gas or burning. The switch is usually at the top of the basement stairs or near the unit. A gas smell means leave the building, call 911 and the gas utility from outside, then call us. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone outdoors and call 911.

Do not remove covers, adjust the burner or touch hot pipes. Steam can scald in seconds.

What to do in the first hour

  1. Switch off the burner. Use the emergency switch or the thermostat setting, never the water supply, to stop heating first.
  2. Let it cool. Wait before touching anything. The shell and pipes stay hot well after the burner stops.
  3. Photograph the gauge and sight glass. Pressure and water level help the plumber understand what happened.
  4. Catch the water. Place a pan or towels under drips and move stored items away from the base.
  5. Do not refill the boiler yourself. Feeding cold water into a hot, dry boiler can crack it. Wait for the plumber.
  6. Call the 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher where you see water and what the gauge reads.

Why steam heat shows up in older Burlington County houses

Local housing note

Most of Mount Laurel's housing was built after the mid 1960s, so steam boilers are less common here than in older towns. If you live in an older house, such as one in the Moorestown Historic District or in the Mount Holly Historic District, the chance of a steam system rises.

That gives you a practical shortcut. If your home is newer, confirm what you have before ordering parts, because hot water boiler fixes will not help a steam boiler. If it is an older house, ask whether the radiator piping is original, since aging pipe is a common leak source. Our team serves Moorestown and Mount Holly as well as Mount Laurel.

What a steam boiler check should cover

  • Water level in the sight glass compared to the mark on the boiler.
  • Condition of the gauge, low water cutoff and relief valve.
  • Leaks at joints, valves and the base of each radiator.
  • Combustion check of the burner and flue, to confirm safe venting.
  • Condition of the water, since rusty or oily water speeds corrosion.
  • Pressure when the system is running.

A plumber who skips the combustion and venting check has skipped the part that matters most for safety.

Repair or replace the boiler

A boiler with a leaky gauge glass, a worn valve or a single bad joint is a good repair candidate. A cracked boiler section or a shell that is leaking at the base is a different story, because those leaks cannot be patched safely. Replacement is also worth discussing when the boiler needs repeated repairs and the piping around it is wearing out too.

When a pipe fails and floods a ceiling, burst pipe repair may come first, followed by a boiler review. Ongoing work on the unit itself falls under our boiler repair service.

What shapes a steam boiler leak bill

No fair number exists without seeing the boiler. These are the pieces that set the price.

  • Leak source. A gauge washer is minor, a boiler section is major.
  • Boiler age and parts. Older steam boilers may need parts that take time to source.
  • Pipe condition. Corroded supply and return piping can add repairs beyond the first leak.
  • Safety work. Combustion and venting corrections are part of the job when they are needed.
  • Time of the call. Nights, weekends and holidays are priced as emergency work.

Insurance may treat a sudden boiler failure and a slow leak differently, so ask your agent before assuming a slow leak is covered.

Prepare before the cold weather arrives

A leak found in September costs less than one found during a January cold snap. Have the boiler looked over before the heating season, particularly if it leaked last winter. Ask about the water level, the cutoff device and the condition of vents. Residents of Evesham who inherited a steam system with a house should do the same.
