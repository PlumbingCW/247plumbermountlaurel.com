Garbage Disposal Installation in Mount Laurel: Steps, Safety and Limits

Swapping a disposal is a doable afternoon project if a unit is already there. Adding one where none existed is a bigger job.

By the Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing team · Published August 26, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026.

Read this before you pick up a wrench

A disposal has an electric motor and sits under a wet sink. Turn off its power at the breaker, not just the wall switch, and test the wires with a non-contact tester before you touch them. If you are not sure which breaker feeds it, do not guess. Stop and call a licensed plumber or electrician.

If the kitchen floor is wet and you cannot reach the panel safely, stay out and call 911.

Seven steps to swap an existing disposal

  1. Kill the power. Switch the breaker off, unplug a corded unit or open the wire cover and test that the leads are dead.
  2. Disconnect the plumbing. Put a bucket under the trap. Loosen the drain elbow, the dishwasher hose and the discharge tube.
  3. Remove the old unit. Support the unit's weight, twist the mounting ring off its lugs and lower it out.
  4. Prepare the sink flange. If you reuse the flange, scrape off old putty. If not, remove and reseat it with fresh plumber's putty and the new gasket.
  5. Hang the new unit. Snap the mounting ring on, lift the disposal onto it and twist until the lugs lock.
  6. Wire and connect. Match black, white and green leads at the box or plug in the cord. Reattach the discharge, elbow and dishwasher hose, and knock out the dishwasher plug first on a new unit.
  7. Test with water. Restore power, run cold water, flip the switch and look for leaks at the flange, elbow and hose for several minutes.

Code and permit questions in Mount Laurel

Local code note

New Jersey's Uniform Construction Code adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code for plumbing work, and the plumbing subcode official in your town is the authority on how it is applied. Whether a disposal swap or a new install needs a permit depends on the scope.

The payoff is a short phone call before the work, not after. A same size swap is usually simpler than adding a first-time circuit, and the office can tell you what they expect for each. Having it sorted keeps a sale or inspection from stalling later.

Hard water, scale and what goes down the drain

The township's water quality summary lists total hardness from 63 to 138 ppm across the supplies it names. The MUA and New Jersey American Water sit near 80 to 100 ppm. Scale does not stop a disposal from running, but mineral buildup in drain lines narrows them over time, and a slow kitchen drain makes a disposal clog more often. Run cold water during grinding and for 15 seconds after to carry waste past the trap.

Skip grease, bones, fibrous stalks and coffee grounds in bulk. The unit is a grinder with no blades, using a spinning plate, so hard or stringy waste can jam it or stick in the trap.

Parts and tools to have on the counter

  • A new disposal sized for your sink flange and cabinet depth.
  • Plumber's putty and the mounting kit that came with the unit.
  • A power cord kit if the unit is hardwired, or an outlet if it uses a plug.
  • Channel lock pliers, a screwdriver set and a bucket.
  • A non-contact voltage tester.
  • Wire nuts and electrical tape rated for the job.
  • A flashlight and old towels for the cabinet.

When to stop and call

Stop if the old unit is hardwired and you cannot test the circuit. Stop if the cabinet shows rot or mold, if the drain is slow even with the disposal removed, or if any wire insulation is cracked. Water and power under a sink do not give you room for guesswork.

A plumber can handle garbage disposal repair when the existing unit can be saved, and a full plumbing installation when a new drain or line is needed.

What shapes the cost of a professional install

A swap and a first-time install are very different jobs. These items move the price.

  • New or replacement. A swap uses the existing mount and wiring, while a first install needs both.
  • Unit horsepower and quality. Heavier duty models cost more and need more room.
  • Electrical work. A new switched outlet or circuit adds a separate task.
  • Drain condition. Corroded or mismatched drain parts get replaced during the job.
  • Dishwasher connection. Adding an air gap or high loop takes extra fittings.

If a leaking disposal has already damaged a cabinet, ask your insurance agent how your policy treats it.
