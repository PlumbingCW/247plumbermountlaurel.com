How to Unclog a Clogged Toilet in Mount Laurel, NJ

Most clogged toilets clear with the right plunger and a calm approach. Here is the order to try things in, and the signs that the clog is not in the toilet at all.

By the Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 2, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026.

Call (609) 547-1417

Do these two things before you plunge

A toilet that is filling toward the rim needs to stop first, and it does not need another flush. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the shutoff valve on the wall behind the bowl by turning it clockwise. This keeps the bowl from overflowing while you work.

Then lay towels on the floor and put on gloves. If the water level is low, you have time. If it is rising, act fast and take your time with the plunger, because rushing makes splashes.

Try these in this order and stop as soon as it drains

Each step is gentler than the next, so work down the list.

  • Use a flange plunger, the kind with a rubber lip that fits the drain opening, not a flat sink plunger.
  • Seat it fully, push down gently first to push out the air, then pull and push firmly for about 20 seconds.
  • Pour a cup of dish soap into the bowl, add hot (not boiling) tap water, and wait 15 minutes before plunging again.
  • Run a closet auger, which is a short toilet snake with a protective sleeve, and turn the handle as you feed it in.
  • Retrieve any visible object, such as a toy, if you can see it near the opening.
  • Stop and call if three or four tries leave the water level unchanged.

Boiling water can crack porcelain, so use hot tap water only.

Wipes marked flushable usually do not break down the way toilet paper does, so they are a frequent cause of repeat clogs.

When it is not just the toilet

If the bathtub gurgles when you flush, the sink drains slowly at the same time, or water rises in a basement floor drain, the clog is likely farther down the line. Stop using water in the house and call. More plunging will not help, and extra flushing can send waste into the lowest fixture.

What a plumber does that a plunger cannot

A plumber can pull the toilet off its flange to clear a stuck object, run a sewer cable through the main line, or send a camera down to see the pipe itself. Roots, a belly in the line or a cracked pipe will keep bringing the clog back until the cause is fixed. We cannot know which it is until the camera goes in.

Persistent trouble with one toilet is a job for toilet repair, while a backup that affects several drains calls for drain cleaning on the main line. If roots or a belly are suspected, sewer line cleaning is the next step. The same applies in Cherry Hill and Mount Holly.

Stopping the next clog before it starts

Flush only toilet paper and human waste. Keep a small wastebasket by the toilet for wipes, cotton swabs and feminine products. Teach children that toys and extra paper do not belong in the bowl.

If the toilet is older, a weak flush can be a design problem, not a habit problem. A plumber can check the fill valve, the flapper and the rim jets to see whether the toilet itself is the cause.
