What Sump Pump Installation Costs in Mount Laurel, NJ

Sump pump installation cost depends on the job more than the pump. A swap in an existing pit and a new pit with drainage are very different days of work, so this guide covers what moves the number.

By the Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing team · Published September 23, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026.

The honest answer on sump pump installation cost

The average cost of a sump pump installation has no single answer, because three different jobs share the same name. One is a pump swap in a pit that already exists. Another is a new pit cut into the slab. The third adds drainage pipe along the walls and often a backup pump.

We do not print dollar figures on this site because a number copied from somewhere else would be wrong for your basement. What we can do is explain what makes each job larger or smaller, so that when you call (609) 547-1417 you can follow the quote and compare it fairly.

Three jobs that get called the same thing

Swap in an existing pit

Lowest effort. The old pump comes out, a new pump and check valve go in, and the discharge line is tested. Best when the pit and pipe are in good shape.

New pit in the slab

Middle effort. Concrete is cut, soil is dug, a liner is set and the discharge line is run outside. Time and cleanup are the main costs.

New pit with drainage and backup

Highest effort. Pipe along one or more walls, gravel, and a second pump or battery system add materials and labor on top of the pit.

Five things that push the price up or down

Two quotes can differ a lot and both be reasonable. Look at what each one includes before you look at the total.

  • Concrete and digging. Cutting a slab, breaking it out and hauling away dirt is the biggest labor item on a new pit. A swap skips most of it.
  • Pump type and size. A primary pump sized for your inflow costs less than a heavy duty unit or a pair of pumps. Bigger is not better if the pit is small.
  • Drainage extent. Each wall that needs pipe and gravel adds material, digging and patching. A single wet corner is cheaper than the whole perimeter.
  • Backup power. A battery system or second pump adds equipment and setup. It also adds testing and replacement later, which is a cost over time.
  • Discharge route. A short run through the rim joist is simple. A long run, a trench or an awkward exit takes more pipe, more labor and a call to 811 first.

Insurance is a separate question. Homeowners policies often treat sudden and accidental damage differently from gradual leaks or wear, and many policies treat groundwater and sump failures separately, so read your policy or ask your agent before assuming anything is covered.

You get a clear price before work starts, and you should ask what the quote includes. To price your own pit, call the 24/7 line and describe where the water enters.

Why backup power belongs in the price conversation

Irene and the power question

During Tropical Storm Irene in late August 2011, water backed up onto streets and into basements near the north branch of Pennsauken Creek, mainly in Ramblewood and Laurelwood. Power then stayed out in Canterbury, Ramblewood, Masonville, Rancocas Woods and Fellowship, and the township utilities authority ran generators to keep its sewage pumps going.

A pump with no electricity is a pit full of water. If you live in one of those sections, ask any plumber to price the backup option separately so you can decide whether it fits your budget instead of finding it buried in a bundle.

Questions that make quotes comparable

Ask each plumber the same things and write the answers down.

  • Is this a swap, a new pit, or a pit with drainage pipe?
  • What size and type of pump is included, and does it have a check valve?
  • Where will the discharge line exit, and how far does it run?
  • Is a backup pump or battery included, or priced separately?
  • Who patches the concrete, and is cleanup part of the quote?
  • Who checks local permit rules? Permit rules vary, so ask the Mount Laurel construction office.

Where saving money backfires

Buying the cheapest pump and skipping the check valve is the classic false saving. Without the valve, every pump cycle lets water fall back into the pit and start the pump again, which wears the motor. A quote that leaves out the discharge route can also hide the largest line item.

If your pump is failing right now, sump pump repair may fix a stuck float or a clogged intake for far less effort than a full replacement. When the pit itself keeps flooding, basement waterproofing is the larger conversation. Neighbors in Cherry Hill and Medford weigh the same choices.
