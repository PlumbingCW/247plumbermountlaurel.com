Frozen Pipe Repair in Mount Laurel, NJ Homes

If a faucet runs dry in a hard freeze, the pipe has probably frozen but not yet split. Here is how to thaw it slowly, how to check for damage, and when to hand it to a plumber.

By the Mount Laurel 24/7 Plumbing team · Published July 8, 2026 · Updated October 15, 2026.

Call (609) 547-1417

Thaw it slowly and keep the faucet open

When water stops at one tap during freezing weather, ice has formed somewhere in the line between the shutoff and that faucet. Frozen pipe repair that does not involve a split is mostly patience and gentle heat. Open the tap first so melting ice and steam have a way out.

Start on the faucet side of the frozen stretch and work back toward the shutoff. A hair dryer on low, a heating pad wrapped around the pipe, or towels soaked in hot tap water all work. Expect it to take an hour or more before full flow returns.

Stay close while you do it. A pipe that cracked when it froze will not leak until the ice melts, and you want to be standing next to the main shutoff when that happens.

Five moves from a dry faucet to running water

  1. Find the main shutoff and turn it once. Confirm the valve actually moves before you need it in a hurry. A stuck valve is something you want to learn about now.
  2. Open the affected faucet, hot and cold. An open tap relieves pressure behind the ice and shows you the moment water starts to move.
  3. Look along every exposed pipe. Check the basement, garage walls, crawlspace and the cabinet under the sink for bulges, cracks or white frost. Splits often show before any water does.
  4. Warm the pipe a little at a time. Work from the tap toward the cold section. Never rush it with a flame, because a pipe heated unevenly can fail at the hot spot.
  5. Run water and watch for ten minutes. When flow returns, let it run, then check the pipe, the ceiling below it and the wall behind it for drips or darkening paint.

Three habits that turn a freeze into a flood

Do not use a torch, propane heater or any open flame on a pipe. Wood framing behind a wall catches fire, and a heater in a closed space can produce carbon monoxide. If the pipe is near the electric panel, an outlet or the water heater, keep standing water away from anything electrical and switch off that breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Do not keep heating a pipe that has started to leak. Close the main shutoff, open a low faucet to drain the line, and call the 24/7 line.

Copper tends to split along its length when ice expands. Plastic lines are more forgiving, but fittings and joints can still fail.

Why some Mount Laurel pipes freeze and others never do

Freezing is almost always about where a pipe runs, not how new the house is. Lines in an exterior wall, an unheated garage, a crawlspace or the rim of a basement lose heat first. A kitchen sink on an outside wall is a classic spot, because the cabinet door keeps warm room air away from the pipes.

The same steps apply from Mount Laurel to Moorestown and Cherry Hill. If a freeze has already cracked a line, a plumber will treat it as a burst pipe repair job, which means cutting out the damaged section and replacing it, not just patching.

Some splits stay hidden inside a wall. In that case a pressure check or a listening tool can find the leak before you open any drywall, and that is the point of leak detection after a hard freeze.

Cheap fixes that keep the next freeze away

  • Disconnect garden hoses and shut off the indoor valve to outdoor spigots before the first hard freeze.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls when the forecast drops.
  • Let a faucet drip on the coldest night, using the tap farthest from the main shutoff.
  • Wrap exposed pipes in a garage or crawlspace with foam sleeves.
  • Seal air leaks around pipes where they pass through the rim joist.
  • Keep the thermostat steady, even when you travel.

None of this is a guarantee against freezing, but together they remove the most common causes.

Where a homeowner should stop and call

Call if you can see a split, if water is coming through a ceiling or wall, if the frozen section is behind a wall you cannot open, or if the main shutoff will not turn. Call too if you thawed the pipe and the pressure or flow stays weak. That can mean a second freeze point or a partial crack.

A plumber cannot always tell by phone whether a pipe has split, so expect a visual check and a pressure test before anyone says the line is sound.
