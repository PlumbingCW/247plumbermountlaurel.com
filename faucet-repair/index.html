The faucet that will not turn off

A handle that spins freely, a stream that will not stop or a stem that has blown out is the true emergency in faucet repair. Go straight to the shutoff valves under the sink and turn each oval handle clockwise. If those valves are frozen or missing, close the main water shutoff and call (609) 547-1417, because the line is staffed on nights, weekends and holidays.

Faucet repair, defined by what actually wears out

Faucet repair is the work of replacing the internal seals, cartridges, seats and supply connections that stop water at a spout or a valve. The fixture body usually lasts far longer than the parts inside it. That is why many dripping faucets are saved with a short repair instead of a new fixture.

A drip that loses a few cupfuls a day also adds up on the water bill over a month. Fix it before it carves a groove in the valve seat, since a damaged seat turns an easy repair into a replacement.

Scale, aerators and the water we draw here

The township's water quality summary lists 63 to 138 ppm of total hardness across the supplies it names. The MUA and New Jersey American Water sit near 80 to 100 ppm. At the high end that is hard enough to crust the aerator screen at the spout tip and stiffen the cartridge inside a single lever faucet. A faucet with weak or sputtering flow often needs only a cleaned aerator, which soaks free in vinegar in a few hours.

If the handle has become stiff or the stream splits sideways, scale on the cartridge is the likely cause. Cleaning or replacing it restores smooth movement, and it spares the rest of the faucet from fighting stuck parts.

Water that will not stop at a faucet or valve

If the water cannot be stopped at the fixture, cut it off upstream while you reach us. The 24/7 line is open on nights, weekends and holidays.

  1. Close the stop valves. Look under the sink for two small oval handles and turn them clockwise to the stop.
  2. Shut the main if those fail. The house main is usually near the water meter or where the line enters the basement.
  3. Open a low faucet. Opening a basement or outdoor tap drains pressure from the pipes and slows the leak upstairs.
  4. Protect the cabinet. Move chemicals and bins out, and set towels or a pan under the leak.
  5. Call for a plumber. Call (609) 547-1417 and mention if any valve would not close, since that changes the repair.

Shower valves, tub spouts and the fixtures behind the wall

Bathroom fixtures hide their working parts, so symptoms matter.

Shower drips after you turn it off

This is typically a worn cartridge or seat in the valve behind the trim. The plumber removes the handle and trim ring to reach it.

Water only runs hot or only cold

A balancing spool or mixing cartridge has failed or scaled over. It is a cartridge swap in many valves.

Tub spout leaks or the diverter sticks

The diverter inside the spout can wear out, and the spout may simply need replacing.

When the supply line, not the faucet, is the leak

Wet cabinets are often blamed on the faucet when the connection underneath is the culprit.

  • Water collects at the base of the cabinet but the spout is dry, which points to a supply line.
  • A braided line shows rust-colored stains or fraying near the nut.
  • The stop valve weeps at the handle stem when you move it.
  • A plastic coupling nut looks cracked or white with mineral crust.
  • Older rigid copper or chrome lines look stiff, pitted or pulled out of position.
  • Drips only appear when the faucet is running, which can mean the sprayer hose is leaking.

Replacing both braided lines and the old stop valves while the faucet is open is common practice, because the parts are cheap compared with a flooded cabinet.

Repair the part or replace the whole fixture

A repair makes sense when the finish is intact, the body is not cracked, and replacement parts exist for the model. Replacement makes sense when parts are no longer made, the body is corroded through or pitted, or the faucet has been repaired several times already. A fixture that sprays from a crack in the body cannot be fixed with seals.

Older fixtures in any house are the ones where a repair kit can be hard to find, and replacement lets you also add a new stop valve. If you are adding a fixture in a new place, our plumbing installation page covers that work.

What decides the cost of a faucet or valve job

A faucet repair can be quick or involved depending on what is behind the handle. The plumber can usually narrow it after a short look.

  • Fixture type and brand. A common cartridge is easy to source, while a discontinued model may mean replacement.
  • Valve location. A kitchen spout is open to reach, but a shower valve may need trim removed or wall access.
  • Condition of the stops. Seized shutoff valves add work because they must be replaced before the repair is safe.
  • Repair or new fixture. Supplying and installing a new faucet raises the total compared with a seal change.
  • Hour of the call. Dispatch on nights, weekends and holidays is available, and timing can affect price.

Your insurance policy may treat sudden and accidental damage differently from gradual leaks or wear, so read it or ask your agent before assuming this is covered.

Keep your sink shutoff working

The oval stop valve under a sink is the cheapest insurance in the house, but only if it moves. Turn each one gently a quarter turn every so often so scale does not lock it. If it leaks when turned, stop and have it replaced rather than cranking it.
