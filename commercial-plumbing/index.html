When the building is open and the water is not behaving

Commercial plumbing in Mount Laurel covers repairs and planned work in buildings where many people share the same pipes. A burst supply line in a tenant suite, a backed up restroom or a dead water heater in a kitchen all hurt revenue by the hour. The 24/7 line is staffed on nights, weekends and holidays, so you can call the moment you see trouble.

While you wait, find the shutoff that serves the problem area and close it. Move stock, paper and electronics off the floor, and keep staff away from wet outlets and floor drains that are backing up.

Who pays, the tenant or the landlord

This depends on the lease, and a plumber cannot settle it for you. As a general pattern, fixtures and lines serving only one suite often fall to that tenant, while risers, main lines and shared drains often fall to the owner. Leases differ a lot, so read yours or ask the property manager before approving work.

In an emergency the sensible order is to stop the damage first and sort out billing second. Tell the plumber who is authorizing the work so the invoice goes to the right place.

The jobs commercial buildings call about

Each of these behaves differently in a building than in a house.

Restaurant grease and floor drains

Kitchen drains clog with fat, and a full grease trap can push wastewater out of a floor drain. The fix is cleaning the trap and clearing the line, and hydro jetting may follow. See hydro jetting for when that tool fits.

Flushometer toilets and urinals

These valves run on line pressure, so a weak flush or a constant hiss usually means a worn diaphragm or a stuck stop. Parts are standard and repairs are often quick.

Water heaters at scale

Commercial tanks are larger and sometimes run in banks. A single failed unit may be isolated while the rest keep supplying hot water. Details sit with water heater repair.

Backflow assemblies

A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from flowing back into the supply. If one leaks or fails a test, the plumber can repair or replace it. Testing rules vary, so ask the township.

Hidden leaks in slabs and walls

A water bill that climbs with no visible cause points to a buried leak. The process is covered under leak detection.

Flooding or sewage in a business tonight

A flooded back room or a restroom overflowing at closing time needs a plumber right away. The line is open 24/7 and the plumber who responds is licensed and insured.

  1. Close the closest shutoff. Use the valve serving that fixture first, then the suite valve, then the building main if water keeps coming.
  2. Cut power to wet areas. If water is near panels, outlets or equipment, turn off that breaker only if you can reach it dry.
  3. Photograph before moving anything. Take wide and close shots of the source, the water line on walls and damaged stock.
  4. Call the property manager. If you rent, tell them early so they can authorize work on shared lines.
  5. Call (609) 547-1417. Give the street, the suite number and what you see so the plumber arrives with the right parts.

If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 and the gas utility from outside, and then call us.

What a property manager should keep on file

A little preparation turns a bad night into a short one.

  • A floor plan with every shutoff valve marked and numbered.
  • The building main shutoff location, tested at least once a year.
  • Photos of the water heater, backflow assembly and floor drains in normal condition.
  • A list of after-hours contacts for each tenant and the owner.
  • The lease language on plumbing responsibility, in a place anyone on duty can find.
  • Dates of the last grease trap cleaning and any backflow test.

What moves the price on a commercial call

Commercial work ranges from a quick valve swap to a full line replacement, so no single number fits. The factors below do most of the moving.

  • Access. Pipes above ceilings or behind finished walls take longer to reach than exposed mechanical room lines.
  • Hours. Work at night or on a holiday can cost differently from a daytime visit.
  • Equipment size. Larger valves, tanks and assemblies mean heavier parts and more labor.
  • Scope of damage. A clean repair costs less than one that needs wall, ceiling or flooring work afterward.
  • Shutoff availability. If a suite has no working valve, the plumber may need to close a larger section first.

Business insurance and the lease may each assign a water loss to a different party, so keep photos and the plumber's written notes for your agent and your landlord.

One habit that saves the most trouble

Run each restroom and kitchen fixture once a week in any space that sits unused, such as a seasonal shop or a vacant suite. Dry traps let sewer gas into the room, and stagnant lines can hide small leaks for months. A five minute walk-through is cheaper than finding a stain on a Monday morning.
