Where Ramblewood sits inside Mount Laurel

Ramblewood is an unincorporated community inside Mount Laurel Township, and the Census Bureau treats it as a census-designated place. The 2020 count was 6,655 residents and 2,718 housing units across about 3.4 square miles. Laurel Acres Park and Ramblewood Country Club anchor the area.

What Tropical Storm Irene showed Ramblewood

Documented in August 2011

In late August 2011, Irene pushed water onto streets and into basements along the north branch of Pennsauken Creek, mainly in Ramblewood and Laurelwood. Firefighters alerted about 162 households and officials recommended a voluntary evacuation. Township-wide damage was described as minimal.

The practical lesson is the power. A sump pump stops when the lights go out, and a storm is when you need it most. If your pump is old or you have never lifted the float to test it, a sump pump repair visit before the next storm is cheaper than the cleanup.

When the street main fails, the lowest drain tells you first

In January 2016 a sewer main broke in the Ramblewood section, and crews worked about 24 hours before service came back at 3 a.m. the next day. We have no record of which homes were affected, and none is claimed here. The point is where trouble shows when a main fails: at the lowest drain in the house.

Find your cleanout before you need it, and keep a note of where it is. If sewage surfaces in a basement drain, stop using water and call the Mount Laurel utilities authority about the main. If only your own drain is slow, drain cleaning or a sewer line cleaning visit is the likelier fix.

Before you call from a condo or townhome

Shared walls change who fixes what. A few minutes of checking saves a second visit.

  • Find the shutoff valve under the fixture or at the unit's main before water spreads.
  • Note whether the stain is on your ceiling, a neighbor's, or a common wall.
  • Ask building management who owns the pipe, because the answer differs from one association to the next.
  • Photograph the damage and the date before anything is moved.
  • Check your association's rules on repair hours and elevator or parking access.
  • Have the water heater's age and location ready, since many condos tuck it into a closet.

Water heaters and faucets in Ramblewood homes

A water heater that rumbles, leaks at the base or runs out fast is a common reason for a call. A water heater repair fixes a bad element or thermostat, while a tank leaking from the seam has to be replaced.

A pinhole leak behind a wall is a job for leak detection before anyone cuts drywall. Public water comes from the Mount Laurel utilities authority, with New Jersey American Water serving part of the township, so ask which one bills you.
