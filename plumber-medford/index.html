Medford's plumbing year starts with the first hard freeze

Medford covers 39.8 square miles, so housing runs from village streets to outlying roads, and the pipe runs inside those homes vary just as much. After a hard freeze, common problems are lines in unheated garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls. A frozen pipe is not yet a burst pipe, which is why the first hour matters.

Open the cabinet doors under sinks on cold exterior walls and let a thin stream run on the worst nights. If a line is frozen but not split, warm air from a hair dryer is safe and an open flame is not. If you see a bulge, a seam split or water on the floor, close the main valve and call the 24/7 line for burst pipe repair, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Thaw, spring rain and the sump pit that sat idle all winter

When the ground thaws and spring rain arrives, groundwater presses on basement walls and a pump that has been dormant since fall has to work again. Pour a bucket of water into the pit before the wet weeks and watch the float rise and the pump push water out. A pump that hums without moving water, will not start, or cycles nonstop needs sump pump repair before the next storm, not after it.

Power loss is the quiet failure. After Tropical Storm Irene in 2011, outages lingered in parts of neighboring Mount Laurel, and a pump with no power moves nothing. A battery backup or a generator plan is worth a conversation if your basement has ever taken water.

A fall check for the water heater before the cold sets in

Summer fills houses with guests, laundry and irrigation, and the tank in the basement works harder than anyone notices. Before the first cold snap, walk through these.

  • Read the age label on the tank and write the date down where you will find it again.
  • Look at the floor around the base for rust flakes, drips or a damp ring.
  • Check the relief valve discharge pipe for staining, which can mean it has been opening.
  • Listen for popping or rumbling when the burner or element runs, a sign of sediment.
  • Note whether the hot water runs out faster than it did last winter.
  • On a gas unit, confirm the pilot or igniter lights without delay and the flame looks steady.

A tank with a slow leak at the seam cannot be patched, while a failed thermostat or igniter usually can. Our plumbers will tell you which case you have, then quote water heater repair or a replacement and explain the price before any work begins.

Eight wells, iron sequestering and what it means for your fixtures

Medford Township water system

Medford's public system, listed as PWSID NJ0320001, runs eight active wells in the upper PRM and Mount Laurel-Wenonah aquifers. It also buys water from New Jersey American Water. The groundwater is treated with an iron-sequestering agent and chlorine, and no fluoride is added.

Sequestering holds dissolved iron in suspension instead of filtering it out, so some iron can still settle as sediment in a heater tank or collect in faucet aerator screens. That is why a heater flush matters here, and why a sudden drop in flow at one faucet is often a clogged aerator, not a failing pipe. If water turns rust colored at every tap, call us and also ask the township what changed.

The township's 2025 report lists a 2023 lead result of 0 ppb at the 90th percentile. That describes the system, not your service line, so ask what yours is made of. The report gives no hardness figure we can quote, so we will not guess one. If you draw from a private well instead of the township system, treatment and testing are your responsibility.

Village homes, old glass-works country and the outlying roads

Historic Medford Village is lined with Main Street shops and historic homes, and the township's glass industry ran from about 1825 to about 1925. A house with that much history can carry galvanized supply pipe, cast iron drains and clay lines under the yard. Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, which shows up as low pressure and brown water, and repiping is the answer only when the damage is widespread, not when one section has failed.

Part of Medford sits in the Pinelands National Reserve and the Pinelands Area. For new outdoor lines or digging, ask the Medford construction office what applies, and call 811 before any shovel goes in.

If your home is on septic instead of public sewer

We do not know which streets are sewered, so this is conditional. If your home is on a septic system rather than public sewer, slow drains in every room, gurgling and wet ground over the tank point to the system, not a clog in one fixture line. A full tank is a job for a septic contractor, and chemical drain cleaner will not fix it.

If only one sink or tub is slow, the problem is a branch line and drain cleaning is the right tool. Describe the symptoms when you call and the dispatcher will tell you honestly which kind of job it is.

Before we arrive in Medford

A few minutes of work in your own house makes the visit faster and keeps damage down.

Find your shutoff

On the township system, the main is usually where the line enters near the meter or basement wall. On a private well, look near the pressure tank and switch off the well pump breaker.

Cut power near water

If water is reaching the sump pit, panel or outlets, turn off those breakers only if you can reach the panel dry. Never stand in water to do it.

Clear the way

Move boxes and stored items off the basement floor, away from the heater, the pit and any septic or sewer cleanout so the plumber can reach them.

Gather the details

Note if it froze overnight, if you are on township water or a well, on public sewer or septic, and the age printed on the heater tank.
